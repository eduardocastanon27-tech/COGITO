You've proven one thing: when someone fills out the form, a row lands in the database. That's real, but it's not what the client asked. He asked "can customers reach me?" Right now the answer is: a customer's request goes into a drawer that **he has no reason to ever open**. If nobody tells him a quote came in, the lead sits there and rots. From the customer's side it looks like they reached him; from his side, silence. That's the worst failure mode — it looks done and quietly loses business.

Saving to the database is the *middle* of the feature, not the end.

## What "done" actually means here

The feature is done when a stranger's request reliably reaches a human who can act on it, and you've watched it happen from the outside. Concretely:

1. **Delivery** — the submission actually gets to the client: an email/text notification, or a dashboard he'll genuinely check. A DB row he never looks at is not "reaching him."
2. **The customer knows it worked** — after submitting they see a clear "thanks, we got it" confirmation, so they don't refill or assume it's broken.
3. **Nothing gets lost on a bad submission** — required fields, an email/phone that's actually usable to reply, and the form doesn't silently eat an error.
4. **It's spam-survivable** — some basic protection, or his inbox becomes unusable and he stops checking (back to lost leads).
5. **Proven from the customer's seat** — you submit a real test from a logged-out, incognito, not-your-machine session on the live site, and you confirm the notification lands where *he* will see it. Testing while logged in as yourself, or only checking the DB, is the classic "owner-can-see ≠ customer-can-reach" trap.
